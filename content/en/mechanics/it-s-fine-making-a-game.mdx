Twenty-four hours. One unstable build. A dog named Joe who happens to be your project manager. That is the premise of **It's Fine**, an idle/incremental game where it's fine making a game — right up until the deadline starts breathing down your neck. You code, you upgrade, and you prestige your way toward a stable version before the clock runs out.

Released on September 23, 2026 by developer Buma Games and published alongside SpaceJazz, It's Fine turned heads fast. Community reports on Steam show a "Very Positive" rating from 358 user reviews, with 88% of players recommending it. The studio also announced that the game sold 10,000 copies in just over 24 hours after launch.

So why does it's fine making a game under fictional crunch feel so satisfying in practice? The answer lives in its mechanics — a layered system where every station evolves instead of just spitting out bigger numbers.

## Why It's Fine Making a Game Is More Than a Punchline

The title is a joke, but the design is serious. It's Fine drops you into the last day of development on an indie project, working under Joe — a demanding dog who runs the schedule. Every action you take feeds a resource pipeline that pushes the build closer to shippable.

That framing matters because it turns a familiar idle loop into a narrative one. Instead of abstract cookies or numbers, you are generating wishlists, wrangling bizarre contraptions, and racing a countdown. According to the official [It's Fine store page on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3936270/Its_Fine/), the game blends casual, indie, simulation, and strategy tags — a mix that reflects how many systems feed into one goal.

## The Core Loop: Code, Upgrade, Sleep, Repeat

At its heart, It's Fine follows a standard incremental rhythm, but each stage of the loop is dressed up with developer-crunch flavor. You spend early minutes clicking and assigning effort, then automate, then reset for permanent gains.

The key design decision is that progression gates new *effects*, not just bigger multipliers. That means the loop keeps changing shape as you grow, which is exactly what separates a memorable idle game from a spreadsheet.

| Loop Stage | What You Do | What It Produces | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early run | Actively work stations and unlock basics | Raw resources and new rooms | Teaches each station's rules |
| Mid run | Scale output and automate tasks | Faster production, more resources | Removes busywork, adds strategy |
| Prestige | Sleep to bank progress | Sleephours currency | Converts a run into permanent power |
| Meta | Spend Sleephours in the Dreamtree | Permanent upgrades and new mechanics | Makes every future run different |

Community reports suggest the prestige step is where most players first feel the game "click." Wiping a run feels bad in most games; here it is framed as a nap, which softens the blow and fits the theme perfectly.

## Evolving Mechanics: Stations That Change as You Grow

This is the section that defines It's Fine's identity. The store description promises "unique evolving mechanics," and the examples are genuinely strange in the best way. Marketing work is handled by a botfarm that churns out wishlists. A legendary Voodoo Kraken can be tamed. And then there is Peanut.

Peanut is a squirrel who generates clean energy — until he evolves and starts firing lightning across the room. That single example tells you everything about the design philosophy: upgrades should create problems as often as they solve them, and the chaos is the reward.

| Station Type | Base Function | Evolved Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing / Botfarm | Generates wishlists for your team | Scales into a wishlist engine feeding launch hype |
| Voodoo Kraken | Tamed legendary entity | Unlocks deeper mechanic layers as it grows |
| Peanut (Squirrel) | Produces clean energy | Evolves and shoots lightning around the room |
| Coding Stations | Push the build toward stability | Unlock new resources and effects as you progress |

Because every station has its own resource and its own evolution path, no two runs feel identical. You are constantly reassessing which system deserves your attention next.

## Prestige and the Dreamtree: How Sleephours Work

Most incremental games use a single prestige currency. It's Fine builds a multi-layered version of that idea around the most valuable resource an indie developer can have: sleep.

You sleep to earn Sleephours, then pour them into the Dreamtree to unlock permanent meta-upgrades, brand-new mechanics, and unique effects that carry across every subsequent run. The deeper you push, the more layers of prestige open up — which means the game keeps rewarding you long after the first reset.

| Prestige Layer | Currency | Persists Across Runs? | Primary Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| First reset | Sleephours | Yes | Core permanent upgrades |
| Dreamtree | Sleephours | Yes | New mechanics and unique effects |
| Deeper layers | Undisclosed in-game | Yes | Advanced meta-progression |

The developers hint that a good night's sleep may not always be enough to hit the deadline — and deliberately leave what happens next for players to discover. That kind of teasing is a smart hook for a genre built on curiosity.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What kind of game is It's Fine?**
It's Fine is an idle/incremental game about surviving the final 24 hours of game development crunch. You build up resources, unlock evolving mechanics, prestige through Sleephours, and try to ship a stable version on time under your dog boss, Joe.

**Is it fine making a game if I'm new to incremental titles?**
Yes. The early loop is simple and forgiving, and the evolving stations introduce complexity gradually. Prestige systems add depth later, so newcomers are not overwhelmed on the first run.

**How long does a full run take?**
The developers have not published a fixed completion time. Community reports suggest the main loop is quick, while the multi-layered prestige system and alternate endings extend play well beyond a single session.

**Does It's Fine contain AI-generated content?**
The studio states that all music is composed by Alon Amit and that the game is written by a husband-and-wife duo. It is marketed as 100% human made with 0% AI music.

**Where can I buy it?**
