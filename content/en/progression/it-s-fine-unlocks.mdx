Every run in *It's Fine* is a race against a 24-hour crunch clock, and the only way to keep pace with that clock is to understand the unlock chain. It's Fine unlocks aren't random rewards — they're gated milestones that expand your station, your automation, and eventually your entire timeline. Miss a trigger and your run stalls; sequence those unlocks well and each cycle finishes faster than the one before it.

This guide maps the full It's Fine unlocks route, from your first room action through late-game automation networks, prestige resets, and hidden ending conditions. It's written for players who would rather follow a plan than babysit another stalled save file.

## How It's Fine Unlocks Work

Unlocks in *It's Fine* are not handed to you on a timer. They respond to what your station is actually producing. The fan-maintained [It's Fine Wiki guides hub](https://www.it-s-fine.wiki/) organizes the game around a repeating loop: develop rooms, collect resources, buy upgrades, rest when progress slows, and reset to convert short-term work into permanent growth.

That loop is the engine behind nearly every unlock. Before a new facility appears, you usually need two things: enough of a specific resource, and a stable enough production chain to support the facility once it exists. This is why players who rush often hit a wall — they unlock a station before they can feed it.

| Unlock Source | What It Gates | Typical Trigger | Stage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Room development actions | First resource income and basic facilities | Using available room actions consistently | Opening minutes |
| Production milestones | Higher-tier facilities like Computer, GPU Rack, and Cloud Servers | Reaching listed resource or output thresholds | Early to mid |
| Facility Overdrive challenges | Throughput and automation bonuses | Building and supplying a facility, then activating Overdrive | Mid to late |
| Sleep resets and Dreamtree | Permanent prestige upgrades | Ending a run through sleep and spending Sleep Hours | Any stage |
| Exploration and special conditions | Secrets, collectibles, alternate endings | Revisiting areas after milestones and testing unusual interactions | After the core route |

## Early It's Fine Unlocks: Rooms, Computer, and Your First Loop

Your first hour is about building a routine, not optimizing it. Start by using room actions to generate income, then spend on upgrades that make that same loop faster. Once resources flow steadily, the Computer facility becomes your first real automation milestone — and the Computer Overdrive challenge teaches you how output, storage, and supply interact.

A common early mistake is buying every cheap upgrade the moment it appears. That spreads your limited resources across improvements that don't touch your current bottleneck. Instead, ask one question each time: what is actually slowing me down right now?

| Step | What You Unlock | Trigger | Mistake to Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | First room actions and resource income | Using available room actions | Spreading early spending across low-impact upgrades |
| 2 | Basic production facilities | Hitting early resource thresholds | Buying downstream upgrades before securing inputs |
| 3 | Computer facility | Meeting construction requirements | Activating it without a stable input supply |
| 4 | Computer Overdrive | Satisfying facility activation conditions | Letting storage fill so output gets blocked |
| 5 | First Sleep reset | Reaching the practical limit of the run | Holding a stalled run hoping for a distant upgrade |

## Mid-Game Unlocks: GPU Rack, Cloud Servers, and Overdrive

Mid-game unlocks widen your processing capacity. The GPU Rack raises throughput, and Cloud Servers let you scale several connected systems at once. Both are easier to unlock when the previous facility is already producing a dependable surplus, because the new station inherits the health of the network around it.

Overdrive challenges are best treated as an ordered route rather than isolated goals. Each completion strengthens the production network that the next challenge depends on. Player experience suggests that attempting Kraken or Peanut before the surrounding facilities are stable mostly results in wasted cycles.

| Order | Facility | Stage | Main Focus | Practical Tip |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| #1 | Computer | Early | First reliable automated loop | Keep the setup conservative and expand only once supply is continuous |
| #2 | GPU Rack | Early to mid | Processing capacity and throughput | Finish it after Computer produces a dependable surplus |
| #3 | Cloud Servers | Mid | Scaling several connected systems | Link multiple reliable inputs and check storage limits first |
| #4 | Kraken | Late | Complex resource dependencies | Improve the weakest connected facility before retrying |
| #5 | Peanut | Late | Specialized objective with full automation | Keep the main economy running while Peanut gets priority |

Before activating any Overdrive, confirm three things: a stable input supply, enough reserve resources to survive a dip, and storage headroom so completed output isn't blocked. Those three checks prevent most failed attempts.

## Late-Game It's Fine Unlocks: Kraken, Peanut, and Timelines

Late progression moves beyond individual runs. Timeline Jump opens a new stage of the game, while Timeline Toxicity adds a planning constraint that punishes careless jumping. The rule of thumb: develop the current timeline as far as it practically goes, then jump when the permanent value clearly outweighs staying.

After a jump, separate what you must rebuild from what carries over. Use the retained systems as scaffolding for a faster rebuild, and recheck your upgrade order — the fastest route after a jump often looks different from your first run.

| Tier | Build | Best For | Core Unlocks Involved | Upgrade Priority |

| A | Overdrive challenge build | Facility challenge objectives | Dedicated workstations, GPU Rack, target facility | Target throughput, input reliability, storage |
| B | Balanced growth station | General progression and resource expansion | Minibots, workstations, Computer, GPU Rack | Reliable production, then processing capacity |
| C | Collection and cleanup build | Collectibles and unfinished achievements | Flexible workstations, reserve storage | Accessibility and low-maintenance automation |

## Prestige, Secret, and Ending Unlocks

Sleep Hours and the sleep reset form the main short-term reset loop. Ending a run converts your active progress into Dreamtree upgrades that persist across every future run. That means a reset is never a loss — it's a conversion. The skill is knowing when the conversion is worth more than continuing.

| Run Stage | Reset Signal | What You Gain | Check First |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Early | Basic loop established, progress slowing | First Dreamtree upgrades | Whether a nearby upgrade costs less than a reset |
| Mid | Next meaningful upgrade feels distant | Compounded permanent bonuses | Whether another active upgrade beats reset value |
| Late | Milestone reached, next one far off | A shorter path to the same milestones | Progress per cycle rather than run length |

Secret and ending unlocks behave differently. They usually require deliberate exploration: revisiting previously visited areas after a major milestone, interacting with elements that seem out of place, or satisfying conditions that the normal production route never demands. If you're chasing alternate endings, keep a separate save before experimenting so you don't disrupt a working station.

A short list of habits that quietly delay unlocks:

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jumping timelines too early | You rebuild from a weaker foundation | Finish current-timeline milestones first |
| Ignoring Overdrive prerequisites | The challenge stalls mid-window | Prepare inputs, storage, and reserves beforehand |
| Over-specializing before collecting | You must rebuild to reach interaction points | Keep routes open and storage flexible |
| Treating all upgrades as equal | Dilutes late-game investment | Favor upgrades that compound across runs |

## It's Fine Unlocks FAQ

### What order should I unlock things in It's Fine?

Follow production first: rooms and basic income, then Computer, GPU Rack, Cloud Servers, Kraken, and Peanut, with Sleep resets woven in whenever a run slows. Timeline Jumps and secret content come after the core route is stable.

### Why isn't my next unlock showing up?

Nine times out of ten, it's a supply problem rather than a missing requirement. Check whether your inputs are continuous, your storage has headroom, and your earlier facilities are still running at full value. A stalled production chain can block a milestone even when your total resources look sufficient.

### Do I lose my It's Fine unlocks when I sleep or jump timelines?

Dreamtree upgrades and other permanent progression remain available after a reset. What you rebuild are the active-run facilities and their output. Community reports consistently recommend resetting earlier than you think, because permanent upgrades compound faster than a stretched-out run.

### How many achievements are tied to unlocking content?

The fan wiki lists 67 Steam achievements, spanning tutorial progression, resource growth, facility Overdrive challenges, collection goals, special conditions, and endgame completion. Most of them unlock naturally if you follow a steady progression route instead of chasing individual objectives.

Track your It's Fine unlocks like a checklist rather than a race. Prepare the input chain before you build, complete Overdrive challenges in order, reset when a run slows, and save the exploration for when your station can run without you. That's how a 24-hour crunch turns into a repeatable, satisfying loop.
