Chasing all of the It's Fine endings can feel like a guessing game until you understand how the game gates alternate outcomes behind progression milestones. Most players don't miss an ending because they lack skill — they miss it because they launched the final sequence too early or changed too many variables at once. This guide lays out a repeatable It's Fine endings route: build the station, finish the baseline path, then test one special condition at a time.

The good news is that the structure is learnable. Once you know which systems feed into ending outcomes, the whole hunt turns from guesswork into a checklist you can actually verify.

## How It's Fine Endings Actually Work

The route structure is built from one normal progression path plus a layer of optional discoveries. You establish the station, push its production systems forward, and reach the standard endgame sequence — that's your baseline. Alternate outcomes branch off from that foundation through hidden interactions, unusual sequences, and special conditions that only become visible once the station has changed enough.

That last part matters more than most players expect. A room that looked ordinary in your first hour may behave completely differently after a major upgrade, an Overdrive challenge, or a Timeline Jump. Ending hunting is therefore less about raw production speed and more about documentation: knowing what you did, in what order, and at which progression state.

Community reports describe the same experience over and over — players who rush the final sequence often find themselves locked out of outcomes they assumed were still open. The fix is simple in principle: treat your first successful run as a reference point, not a race.

| Route Layer | Main Focus | When to Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline progression | Developing rooms, facilities, resources, and upgrades | During your first serious run |
| Facility progression | Inspecting newly unlocked stations and production areas | After major facility milestones |
| Hidden interactions | Revisiting unusual objects and earlier areas | After upgrades, challenges, or timeline changes |
| Special conditions | Testing unusual sequences or optional requirements | Once the core route is stable |
| Alternate outcomes | Comparing completed requirements before the final sequence | Before committing to endgame progression |

### Baseline, Discovery, and Condition Paths

Three route types cover almost everything you will do while working toward It's Fine endings:

- **Baseline route:** Build a reliable production loop, unlock the main progression systems, and reach the standard endgame sequence. This is your control group.
- **Discovery route:** Return to rooms and facilities after major milestones to find interactions that were invisible during the opening cycle.
- **Condition route:** Prepare a separate plan for unusual requirements, special sequences, or choices that may shift which outcome is available.

Run them in that order. A normal route reveals the game's structure without forcing an early commitment to an experimental condition, and once the main systems feel familiar, optional paths become much easier to isolate.

## The Step-by-Step Ending Route Checklist

The key principle here is separating active-run optimization from discovery work. If you try to do both at once, a failed ending condition tells you nothing — you won't know which change broke it.

1. **Establish the first production loop.** Get rooms running and resources flowing before you chase anything optional.
2. **Unlock progression systems.** Move through upgrades, Sleep Hours, Dreamtree improvements, and facility progression. Write down when new rooms, stations, or interactions appear, because later route checks depend on the station's development state.
3. **Complete the baseline sequence.** Push the run far enough to see how the standard outcome concludes. Don't spend an entire run on experiments before you know what "normal" looks like.
4. **Revisit earlier areas.** Return to rooms and facilities after major upgrades, Overdrive challenges, or Timeline Jumps. Inspect stations that previously seemed decorative.
5. **Test one optional condition at a time.** Pick a single unusual interaction, then note the order of actions, the facilities involved, and the progression state required before the event appeared.

| Progression Phase | Primary Objective | Ending Preparation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening cycle | Establish rooms and basic resources | Learn the normal route without experimenting |
| Early development | Unlock upgrades and production facilities | Note new rooms and interactions |
| Mid progression | Improve automation and clear useful challenges | Revisit facilities after major upgrades |
| Timeline progression | Plan Timeline Jumps and manage Timeline Toxicity | Check which discoveries remain available |
| Endgame setup | Finish optional objectives and route requirements | Review the ending checklist before final progression |

Your first route also tells you which resources and facilities are essential for later experiments. A mature, automated layout gives you time to investigate secrets instead of constantly patching production bottlenecks — and that time is what actually makes multiple endings practical.

## Hidden Interactions, Facilities, and Spoiler Levels

Alternate outcomes are tied to hidden interactions, special conditions, and endgame requirements rather than simple resource milestones. Some discoveries only become visible after a facility changes, an Overdrive challenge is completed, or a new timeline opens, which is why rechecking the station counts as part of normal completion rather than optional busywork.

For the community-maintained breakdown this planning method is based on, see the [It's Fine endings route guide and checklist on the game's wiki](https://www.it-s-fine.wiki/endings/It-s-Fine-all-endings). It is a useful companion once your baseline run is done.

If you want to preserve the discovery experience, scale your spoiler exposure:

| Spoiler Level | What to Do | What to Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | Revisit unusual objects and recently changed facilities | Searching every area at random |
| Moderate | Compare facility milestones against new interactions | Assuming every interaction changes an ending |
| Full | Test one known sequence or special condition | Combining several unknown conditions at once |
| Endgame | Review optional tasks before the final sequence | Starting the final route with unfinished requirements |

The most productive places to revisit are the facilities tied to the game's progression and challenge systems:

- **Computer:** Check again after establishing your first automated production loop.
- **GPU Rack:** Inspect it once production scaling changes what the station can support.
- **Cloud Servers:** Review the connected production network after you scale automation.
- **Kraken:** Revisit after major challenges or upgrades shift the station's state.
- **Peanut:** Check once the station is stable enough to maintain specialized production.

None of these are confirmed ending triggers on their own. They are reliable investigation points, because major facility progression is what exposes new events.

## Timeline Jumps and Endgame Readiness

A Timeline Jump deserves special handling. It changes the station's progression context and introduces Timeline Toxicity as an additional planning factor, which means a jump can quietly alter which discoveries remain available to you.

Before jumping, finish practical milestones in the current timeline and note any optional objectives still outstanding. After the jump, rebuild around the new bottleneck instead of automatically repeating your old layout. Check room development, production facilities, Sleep Hours, Dreamtree priorities, and unresolved interactions before you attempt another ending path.

| Checkpoint | Confirmed State | Recommended Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core progression | Rooms and basic facilities are developed | Continue toward the baseline route |
| Resource economy | Production is stable enough for experimentation | Reserve resources for optional interactions |
| Facility milestones | Major stations are unlocked or upgraded | Revisit each changed facility |
| Challenge progress | Relevant Overdrive objectives are complete | Check for newly available interactions |
| Timeline status | Current timeline goals are understood | Decide whether to continue or jump |
| Endgame readiness | Optional requirements are reviewed | Begin the final sequence only after checking your route log |

## Troubleshooting Failed Ending Routes

When a route doesn't produce the expected outcome, resist the urge to restart everything. Compare the failed attempt against your notes first — most misses trace back to timing, not to a hidden bug.

| Check | Question to Ask |
| --- | --- |
| Interaction timing | Was the interaction performed *after* the required milestone? |
| Timeline state | Did a Timeline Jump change which progression state was available? |
| Challenge order | Was a facility challenge completed before the interaction was tested? |
| Variable control | Were several optional conditions changed at the same time? |
| Endgame entry | Did the final sequence begin before optional objectives were finished? |

A simple test log makes this far easier. Record the milestone, the facility, the action, and the outcome for every experiment:

| Attempt | Milestone Reached | Action Taken | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Baseline route complete | Revisited Computer only | No change observed |
| 2 | Baseline + Overdrive cleared | Revisited Computer and Cloud Servers | New interaction appeared |
| 3 | Post–Timeline Jump | Revisited Peanut | Route outcome shifted |

That format turns an uncertain ending hunt into a repeatable investigation, and it protects you from the most common completion mistake: reaching the endgame before checking optional content.

## FAQ: It's Fine Endings

**How many endings does It's Fine have?**
Available guide information confirms a standard progression outcome plus alternate outcomes tied to hidden interactions, special conditions, and endgame requirements, but it does not establish a verified total number of named endings. Use a route checklist to map each outcome you personally confirm.

**Should I finish the normal route before hunting alternate endings?**
Yes. The baseline route gives you a reference point and reveals which later interactions, facilities, or conditions can actually change the progression outcome. Skipping it usually means repeating work.

**Which areas should I revisit for hidden ending routes?**
Return to earlier rooms and facilities after major upgrades, Overdrive challenges, or Timeline Jumps. Computer, GPU Rack, Cloud Servers, Kraken, and Peanut are especially useful checkpoints because they sit closest to the game's progression systems.

**Can a Timeline Jump affect ending progress?**
It can. A jump changes the long-term progression context and adds Timeline Toxicity as a planning factor. Record unfinished optional objectives before you jump, then recheck them after you rebuild.

The final tip is the one that saves the most time: prioritize repeatable notes over guesses. A clear progression log makes hidden interactions, special conditions, and every one of the It's Fine endings far easier to verify.
