## Why the It's Fine Finale Is the Real Test of Your Run

The clock is ticking, Joe the dog is glaring at your build, and everything you have coded comes down to one push: the It's Fine finale. Reaching the It's Fine finale isn't only about surviving the last 24 hours of development crunch — it's the checkpoint that decides which achievements, secrets, and alternate endings are still within reach on this run. Because the game is an incremental simulation built around repeat attempts, the finale works less like a boss fight and more like a report card on how well you used your stations, your sleep, and your Dreamtree upgrades.

That framing matters, because plenty of players treat the finale as a simple finish line. It isn't. It's a gate. Everything you invested — or failed to invest — gets measured in a short window, and the run ends whether you're ready or not.

### What the Finale Actually Asks of You

- **A working production chain.** By the final stretch, coding, upgrade stations, and the game's stranger systems should be feeding each other instead of competing for your attention.
- **Time discipline.** The premise is a 24-hour crunch. Burning that window on a tiny numeric bump rather than a mechanic unlock is the most common way to limp across the line.
- **A plan for what comes next.** Permanent progress carries into future runs, so a strong finale leaves you with more Sleephours and more unlocked systems than the attempt before it.

## What Happens During the Finale: Endings and Outcomes

The finale is where It's Fine stops being a pure numbers exercise and starts asking what kind of developer you were. Shipping a stable build is the headline goal, but the completion structure is much wider: 67 Steam Achievements, hidden secrets, optional challenges, and multiple endings sit on top of that first ship. Community reports consistently describe the first ending as the most forgiving, with later outcomes requiring deliberate, sometimes counterintuitive decisions.

| Outcome | What it requires | Best time to chase it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shipping the game | Complete the core coding and upgrade loop before the crunch timer runs out | First focused run |
| Alternate endings | Vary decisions across runs and explore routes you skipped | After two or three attempts |
| Hidden secrets | Test unusual interactions with evolving stations and side systems | Relaxed exploration runs |
| Challenge objectives | Clear optional goals that layer extra effects onto a run | Once station mechanics feel familiar |
| Full achievement list | All 67 Steam Achievements, many tied to specific decisions | Long-term, multi-run goal |

The practical takeaway: don't try to see everything at once. A finale that ships cleanly is worth more than a finale that chases three endings and finishes none of them.

## Preparing for the It's Fine Finale: Sleephours, Dreamtree, and Prestige

The single most important preparation for the finale happens before the finale. Sleep is the game's prestige mechanic, converting a productive run into Sleephours — described in-game as one of the most valuable currencies an indie developer can have. Those hours go into the Dreamtree, which grants permanent meta-upgrades, new mechanics, and effects that survive every future attempt.

| System | Role in finale prep | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coding | The baseline activity that produces forward motion | Establish it early and keep it steady |
| Upgrade stations | Introduce new resources and mechanical effects | Favor unlocks that broaden the run |
| Sleep | Converts a run into Sleephours | Use when permanent value beats short-term output |
| Dreamtree | Permanent meta-progression across runs | Invest in upgrades that change how a run works |
| Challenges | Optional goals with special effects | Attempt after the base loop is second nature |

A useful mental model is to split every attempt into three phases:

- **Foundation** — build basic production and identify which station is currently capping you.
- **Expansion** — unlock mechanics that add resources, automation, or entirely new systems.
- **Conversion** — decide whether more production is worth less than sleeping for permanent progress.

## Achievements, Secrets, and the Finale Checklist

Sixty-seven achievements is a lot of ground to cover, and the names alone tell you the tone: the list includes entries such as "Giga Chad," "Need For Seed," "Peak Interior," and "Real Civil." Those are rewards for playfulness as much as for grinding, and their exact unlock conditions are best confirmed in-game rather than guessed at from the title.

What you can plan for is the *shape* of the list. Most achievement hunting in It's Fine falls into four buckets:

| Achievement type | What it rewards | How to approach it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Progression milestones | Reaching production or shipping thresholds | Play the core loop naturally |
| Playful discovery | Odd interactions and named characters | Experiment with every station you unlock |
| Prestige and Dreamtree | Repeated runs and meta-upgrades | Sleep often and invest early |
| Endings and completion | Seeing multiple outcomes | Vary decisions between runs |

Because the game's mechanics evolve mid-run, an action that looks pointless at hour two may matter enormously once a station transforms. Keep short notes on when a mechanic changes and what you did immediately before it. That habit turns a lucky secret into a repeatable route.

## A Step-by-Step Route to a Clean Finale

New players often ask how to reach the ending without wasting a run. The answer is sequencing, not speed.

| Phase | Goal | Signal to move on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Learn | Identify resource sources and how stations connect | You can predict your next upgrade |
| 2. Stabilize | Build a production base that runs without constant input | Numbers climb while you plan |
| 3. Diversify | Unlock mechanics that add new resources or automation | You have more options than you can use |
| 4. Convert | Sleep to bank Sleephours and buy Dreamtree nodes | Permanent upgrades outweigh short-term gains |
| 5. Push | Drive toward the ship with the strongest setup you have | The crunch timer is your only limiter |

Steps one through three can comfortably take more than one run. That's expected — It's Fine is designed around the idea that a failed attempt still pays you in meta-progression.

## Common Finale Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buying the biggest visible upgrade | It rarely changes how a run functions | Prioritize unlocks that add mechanics |
| Sleeping too late | You finish with unused potential | Convert once a run stops producing new options |
| Ignoring side systems | Botfarms, the Voodoo Kraken, and Peanut all alter resource flow | Test every system at least once |
| Chasing all 67 achievements immediately | Early attempts lack the tools for secret routes | Bank Dreamtree progress first |
| Restarting after one bad finale | Meta-progression makes later runs easier | Treat the ending as data, not failure |

The through-line is patience. The It's Fine finale rewards players who treat each ending as information rather than a verdict, and the game's evolving systems mean the second attempt is almost always smoother than the first.

## FAQ: It's Fine Finale Questions

**What is the It's Fine finale?**

It's the closing stretch of a run, built around surviving the final 24 hours of an indie development crunch and shipping a stable build. The It's Fine finale also branches into alternate endings depending on the decisions you make along the way.

**How many achievements does It's Fine have?**

The Steam listing shows 67 achievements, alongside hidden secrets, optional challenges, and multiple endings. Many are easier to earn after you've banked a few Dreamtree upgrades.

**Do I need to prestige before finishing a run?**

Not strictly, but sleeping to earn Sleephours and spending them in the Dreamtree makes every subsequent attempt faster and opens mechanics that a first run can't reach.

**Can I see every ending in a single playthrough?**

Community reports suggest no — the endings are tied to different decision paths, so multiple runs are expected. Varying your choices each attempt is the most reliable way to find them all.

Put simply, the finale is not the part of It's Fine you survive. It's the part you plan for. Bank your Sleephours, spend them on Dreamtree nodes that change how a run works, and treat every ending — clean or messy — as one more step toward full completion.
