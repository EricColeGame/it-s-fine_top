## Why Route Order Beats Raw Production Speed

Most players lose their first **It's Fine completion** attempt not because the station is underbuilt, but because they reach the endgame before checking what the run actually unlocked. A clean **It's Fine completion** depends on route order: baseline progression first, optional discoveries second, and a written log of every milestone in between. Get that sequence right, and alternate endings stop feeling like guesswork.

It's Fine layers optional discoveries on top of a normal progression path. Rooms develop, resources flow, upgrades unlock, and the station slowly changes shape. The catch is that many later interactions only become visible once the station has changed significantly, so a route that worked an hour ago may behave differently now.

Community reports keep repeating the same advice: treat your first successful run as a reference point, not a speedrun. Record optional interactions separately so you can tell which discovery actually shifted the outcome.

| Route Layer | Main Focus | When to Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline progression | Rooms, facilities, resources, and upgrades | During your first serious run |
| Facility progression | Newly unlocked stations and production areas | After major facility milestones |
| Hidden interactions | Unusual objects and earlier areas | After upgrades, challenges, or timeline changes |
| Special conditions | Unusual sequences or optional requirements | Once the core route is stable |
| Alternate outcomes | Comparing completed requirements | Before committing to endgame progression |

The reason a normal route should come first is simple: it reveals the game's structure without forcing you to commit early to an experimental condition. Once the main systems feel familiar, optional paths become much easier to isolate and test one by one.

## The Baseline Route: Your First It's Fine Completion

Before chasing a single secret, finish the standard progression path. That first successful run gives you a measuring stick for everything that follows, and it exposes which resources and facilities you will need for later experiments.

1. **Establish the first production loop.** Get basic rooms running and stabilize resource flow before you touch anything unusual.
2. **Unlock progression systems.** Move through upgrades, Sleep Hours, Dreamtree improvements, and facility development. Write down when new rooms or interactions appear, because later route checks depend on the station's development state.
3. **Complete the baseline sequence.** Push the run far enough to see how the standard outcome plays out. Do not burn the entire run on experiments before you know what "normal" looks like.
4. **Revisit earlier areas.** Return to rooms and facilities after major upgrades, Overdrive challenges, or Timeline Jumps. Inspect objects that previously seemed decorative.
5. **Test one optional condition at a time.** Pick a single unusual interaction, then log the action order, the facilities involved, and the progression state required for it to appear.

| Progression Phase | Primary Objective | Ending Preparation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening cycle | Establish rooms and basic resources | Learn the normal route without experimenting |
| Early development | Unlock upgrades and production facilities | Note new rooms and interactions |
| Mid progression | Improve automation and finish useful challenges | Revisit facilities after major upgrades |
| Timeline progression | Plan Timeline Jumps and manage Timeline Toxicity | Check which discoveries are still available |
| Endgame setup | Finish optional objectives and route requirements | Review the ending checklist before the final sequence |

One rule saves hours of frustration: never change several progression variables at the same time. If an ending condition fails, a controlled route makes it obvious which interaction or milestone was missing.

## Timeline Jumps, Overdrive, and Spoiler Discipline

A Timeline Jump deserves more caution than any other system. It changes the station's long-term progression context and introduces Timeline Toxicity as an extra planning factor. Before you jump, finish practical milestones in the current timeline and note which optional objectives still need attention.

After the jump, rebuild around the new bottleneck instead of repeating the old route on autopilot. Recheck room development, production facilities, Sleep Hours, and Dreamtree priorities before attempting another ending path.

| Spoiler Level | What to Do | What to Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | Revisit unusual objects and recently changed facilities | Randomly searching every room |
| Moderate | Compare facility milestones against new interactions | Assuming every interaction changes an ending |
| Full | Test one known sequence or special condition | Stacking several unknown conditions at once |
| Endgame | Review optional tasks before the final sequence | Starting the finale with unfinished requirements |

This is where Overdrive challenges matter too. Completing relevant objectives can expose interactions that were invisible earlier, which is why challenge progress belongs on the same checklist as resource milestones.

## It's Fine Completion Checklist and Troubleshooting

The most common completion mistake is reaching the endgame before optional content has been checked. Because different outcomes may depend on progression order or unusual conditions, treat the route as a series of confirmed milestones rather than a single run.

**Ending route checklist:**

- Complete the baseline progression route
- Unlock and inspect the major production facilities
- Revisit earlier areas after major upgrades or Timeline Jumps
- Finish relevant Overdrive and facility objectives
- Record special interactions and unusual route conditions
- Review unresolved requirements before the final sequence

| Checkpoint | Confirmed State | Recommended Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core progression | Rooms and basic facilities developed | Continue toward the baseline route |
| Resource economy | Production stable enough to experiment | Reserve resources for optional interactions |
| Facility milestones | Major stations unlocked or upgraded | Revisit each changed facility |
| Challenge progress | Relevant Overdrive objectives complete | Look for newly available interactions |
| Timeline status | Current timeline goals understood | Decide whether to continue or jump |
| Endgame readiness | Optional requirements reviewed | Start the final sequence only after checking the log |

When a route fails to produce the expected outcome, resist the urge to restart everything. Compare the attempt against the checklist first.

| Symptom | Likely Cause | What to Try |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ending did not trigger | Interaction performed before its milestone | Repeat it after the next facility upgrade |
| Route felt different after a jump | Changed progression state and Timeline Toxicity | Rebuild around the new bottleneck, then recheck |
| No new events anywhere | Several optional conditions changed at once | Reset to one variable per attempt |
| Finale felt incomplete | Optional objectives left unfinished | Complete the missed objectives, then rerun the sequence |

That habit turns an uncertain ending hunt into a repeatable investigation, and it is the single biggest time-saver in any It's Fine completion plan.

## FAQ: It's Fine Completion

**Q: How many endings does It's Fine have?**

Community guide material confirms a standard progression outcome plus alternate endings tied to hidden interactions and special conditions, but no verified total has been published. Track each outcome you discover in your own It's Fine completion log instead of chasing a number.

**Q: Should I finish the normal route before hunting alternate endings?**

Yes. The baseline route provides a reference point and reveals which later interactions, facilities, or conditions can change the progression outcome.

**Q: Which areas should I revisit for hidden ending routes?**

Revisit earlier rooms and facilities after major upgrades, Overdrive challenges, or Timeline Jumps. Computer, GPU Rack, Cloud Servers, Kraken, and Peanut are the most useful checkpoints.

**Q: Can a Timeline Jump affect ending progress?**

It can. A jump changes the long-term progression context and adds Timeline Toxicity as a planning factor. Record unfinished optional objectives before jumping, then recheck them after you rebuild.
