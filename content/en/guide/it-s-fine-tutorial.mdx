This It's Fine tutorial exists because that premise is funnier — and more stressful — when you actually know what you are doing. ## What It's Fine Actually Is

It's Fine is an active incremental and upgrade clicker built around a single premise: getting version 1.0 out during the final 24 hours of an indie-game crunch. The official site credits Buma Games and directs players to the game's Steam listing, so the Steam page and developer posts are your authority for anything that changes between builds.

That matters more than it sounds. Incremental games live and die on numbers, and numbers get patched. A guide written for one build can quietly become bad advice in another, which is why the most reliable beginner resource — the [It's Fine beginner guide wiki](https://www.itsfine.wiki/its-fine-beginner-guide) — deliberately labels its practical tips as guidance rather than pretending to know unpublished values.

## The Core Loop: Clicking, Upgrades, and Crunch Pressure

Every incremental game runs on the same four-beat rhythm, and It's Fine is no exception. You **generate** a resource by clicking, you **spend** that resource on upgrades, those upgrades **automate** generation so you are not chained to the mouse, and automation lets you **accelerate** into bigger multipliers.

The crunch framing adds a fifth element: a clock. In most idle games, time is a resource you spend freely. Here, time is the antagonist. Your real job is not to click the most — it is to make each remaining minute worth more than the last one.

| Upgrade type | What it usually does | Beginner guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Manual click power | Raises output per click | Strong early, while you are actively clicking |
| Passive or automated generation | Earns while you handle other tasks | The mid-game backbone; scales with time |
| Multipliers | Multiply output you already have | Buy once your base is meaningfully large |
| Cost reducers and efficiency perks | Make future purchases cheaper | Compounds quietly; easy to undervalue |
| One-time unlocks | Open new systems or layers | Always read the tooltip — these change the loop |

Community reports suggest that players who plateau early usually did one of two things: they kept clicking long after automation was available, or they bought multipliers before they had anything worth multiplying. Both are pacing errors, not skill errors.

## A Step-by-Step It's Fine Tutorial Plan for Your First Hour

The table below is a general framework for incremental games with a timer. Use it as a rhythm rather than a schedule, since your build may unlock things in a different order.

| Phase | Goal | Habit to build |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Read the board | Understand what you produce and what the timer governs | Hover everything; read tooltips twice |
| 2. Establish income | Get a steady flow of the game's main resource | Click with intent, not idly |
| 3. Buy rate-changers | Convert clicks into compounding growth | Favor upgrades that change your rate |
| 4. Automate | Reduce manual labor | Free your attention for decisions |
| 5. Specialize | Find the multiplier path that fits your build | Commit to one lane, then measure it |
| 6. Re-evaluate | Compare output per minute, not per click | Write down a number, not a feeling |

A few actionable rules that survive almost any patch:

- **Set a spending threshold.** Decide in advance when you will stop clicking and start buying, then stick to it.
- **Prefer rate over lump sums.** An upgrade that permanently changes how fast you earn beats a one-time boost in nearly every build.
- **Automate the boring part first.** Anything you do on autopilot should be automated before you optimize anything else.
- **Check the clock every upgrade cycle.** Crunch pressure is the mechanic, not decoration.
- **Keep a two-column log.** Left column: what you bought. Right column: what your output did afterward.

## Common Beginner Mistakes and How to Fix Them

Most early frustration in It's Fine is self-inflicted, and it is usually fixable within one session.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Clicking without ever buying | Raw clicks do not compound | Spend at a fixed threshold |
| Buying everything you can afford | Spreads resources thin across weak options | Prioritize upgrades that change your rate |
| Ignoring the timer entirely | The crunch is the core pressure system | Glance at the clock each cycle |
| Following a stale guide | Balance and systems change between builds | Verify against your own save |
| Not writing anything down | You cannot tell what actually worked | Keep a simple two-column log |
| Chasing someone else's "optimal" path | Their build may not match yours | Treat it as a hypothesis to test |

Player experience consistently points to the same conclusion: in timed incremental games, *decisions per minute* matter more than *clicks per minute*. Speed comes from better choices, not faster fingers.

## FAQ

**Is this It's Fine tutorial useful if my build is different from the one described here?**
Yes — the framework holds even when specifics shift. Generate, spend, automate, accelerate, and watch the clock. Adjust the details to match what your build actually offers.

**Do I need to play perfectly to ship version 1.0?**
No. The game is about the crunch, not about flawless execution. Consistent, deliberate decisions beat frantic clicking every time.

**Is It's Fine a good first incremental game?**
It is a reasonable entry point if you enjoy active clicking and a bit of pressure. If you prefer slow, idle-style progression with no timer, the crunch framing may feel stressful rather than fun.

**How often should I re-check official sources?**
Before each session is plenty. Developer posts and the Steam listing are the only places where version-sensitive facts should be treated as confirmed.

The short version of this It's Fine tutorial: read before you click, buy rate over raw power, automate early, and let the clock inform your decisions instead of panicking you. Do that, and version 1.0 stops feeling impossible — it just feels like a very long, very funny day.
