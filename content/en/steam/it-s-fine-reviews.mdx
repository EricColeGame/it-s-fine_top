## Why It's Fine Reviews Are Worth Reading Before You Buy

A game about crunching to ship a game, supervised by a dog named Joe, sounds like a joke — until you actually read the It's Fine reviews and notice how attached players have become. Released on September 23, 2026, this idle/incremental title from developer Buma Games turns the final 24 hours of game development into a resource-management loop. The response has been strong enough that it moved 10,000 copies in roughly a day. If you are deciding whether to spend an evening coding, upgrading, and sleeping for currency, these It's Fine reviews will tell you what you are really signing up for.

### The Premise: 24 Hours, One Dog, One Deadline

It's Fine is a casual indie simulation and strategy game built on incremental progression. You play as a developer racing a deadline, with Joe — a demanding dog who also serves as your project manager — pushing you forward. Every station in the room has its own resources and mechanics, and those mechanics evolve as you grow rather than simply scaling into bigger numbers.

The premise is deliberately absurd. You generate wishlists with a bot farm, tame a legendary Voodoo Kraken, and harness Peanut, a squirrel who produces clean energy until he evolves and starts throwing lightning around the office. Community reports suggest the humor is a major reason players keep coming back after their first prestige.

### The Core Loop: Code, Upgrade, Sleep, Repeat

The rhythm is familiar to idle fans: click or automate a station, convert output into upgrades, then reset for a permanent advantage. What separates It's Fine is the prestige layer. Sleeping earns Sleephours, and the Dreamtree converts them into meta-upgrades, new mechanics, and effects that carry across runs.

Because the prestige system is multi-layered rather than a single multiplier, progression stays interesting longer than in most idle games. That said, players who dislike reset-based loops may find the structure repetitive regardless of how many mechanics unfold.
